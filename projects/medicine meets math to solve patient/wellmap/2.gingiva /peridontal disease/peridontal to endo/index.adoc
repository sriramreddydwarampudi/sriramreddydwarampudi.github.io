The connection between endodontic (pulp-related) and periodontal (gum and bone-related) diseases occurs through anatomical pathways like the apical foramen, lateral canals, and dentinal tubules.




2. Perio → Endo Lesion (Primary Periodontal, Secondary Endodontic)

Starts as periodontitis, causing deep pockets, gum recession, and bone loss.

Bacteria invade the lateral canals or apical foramen, reaching the pulp.

Leads to pulpal necrosis and possible periapical abscess.


Clinical Features

Tooth may be vital initially, but necrosis develops over time.

Deep generalized periodontal pockets.

Widespread bone loss.

Pain is dull, diffuse, and may worsen with biting.


Treatment

Periodontal therapy first, but if pulp necrosis occurs, RCT is needed.



---

3. True Combined Endo-Perio Lesion

Both pulpal and periodontal diseases develop independently and merge.

Severe prognosis, requiring both RCT and periodontal treatment.


Clinical Features

Non-vital tooth.

Deep periodontal pockets and periapical radiolucency.

Severe bone loss.


Treatment

RCT first, followed by scaling, root planing, and possible regenerative therapy.
