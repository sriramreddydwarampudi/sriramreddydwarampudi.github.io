    Here’s the combined version of the 5-Part Classification with objectives and explanations:

    1. Personal Discipline (self-goverance)¶

    (Mastery over the self — mind, body, speech, and daily conduct)

    Self-restraint and Senses¶

    Objective: Controlling desires, emotions, bodily urges Why: Chanakya emphasizes governing the tongue, mind, eyes, and physical impulses. Mastery over one's desires is the foundation of wisdom.

    Cultivation of Inner Virtues¶

    Objective: Develop patience, humility, resilience, and willpower Why: These virtues must be consciously nurtured through daily practice to build strong inner discipline.

    Knowledge, Learning, and Self-Education¶

    Objective: Pursuing lifelong learning and wisdom accumulation Why: Chanakya urges seeking wise teachers and expanding one’s understanding continually.

    Mastery of Speech and Silence¶

    Objective: Knowing when to speak and when to remain silent Why: Deliberate speech and wise silence reflect intelligence and emotional control.

    Daily Habits and Self-Reflection¶

    Objective: Build routines that foster virtue and strength Why: True discipline is reflected in consistent daily routines and the ability to assess and improve oneself regularly.

    2. Human Nature & Psychology(pyschopolitics)¶

    (Understanding others deeply)

    Traits of Friends and Enemies¶

    Objective: Recognizing true allies and hidden foes Why: Friends, enemies, and neutrals each reveal themselves through subtle signs. Chanakya provides insights into reading people accurately.

    Behavior in Crisis¶

    Objective: Understanding how people reveal their true character under pressure Why: Adversity uncovers a person’s hidden traits and intentions.

    Signs of Loyalty and Betrayal¶

    Objective: Identifying trustworthiness through tests and markers Why: Loyalty and betrayal are best understood through actions rather than words.

    Dealing with Fools, Women, and Flatterers¶

    Objective: Navigating weaknesses and vulnerabilities Why: Understanding human flaws allows one to manage relationships with wisdom and tact.

    3. Social Strategy & Relationships(interpersonal politics)¶

    (Mastering social navigation)

    Making and Breaking Alliances¶

    Objective: Building networks and strategic associations Why: Strategic friendships and associations with the virtuous lead to success, while foolish companions bring ruin.

    Diplomacy and Manipulation¶

    Objective: Using tact and persuasion to achieve goals Why: Success often lies in discretion, revealing intentions only when necessary, and using persuasion wisely.

    Social Caution (Who to Avoid)¶

    Objective: Identifying and steering clear of harmful individuals Why: Recognizing and avoiding toxic influences is crucial for maintaining personal stability and peace.

    Marriage and Family Roles¶

    Objective: Understanding duties and dynamics within domestic life Why: The home is a reflection of society. Family roles must be respected for personal and social harmony.

    4. Leadership & Governance(governance)¶

    (Ruling wisely and justly)

    Qualities of an Ideal Ruler¶

    Objective: Embodying attributes required for strong leadership Why: Self-restraint, foresight, and commitment to justice are critical for effective governance.

    Ministerial Conduct and Selection¶

    Objective: Choosing and managing advisors wisely Why: Advisers should be chosen based on merit, loyalty, and wisdom, with constant evaluation of their conduct.

    Role of Punishment and Law¶

    Objective: Using the justice system for order and stability Why: Law enforcement and punishment are essential for maintaining social order, provided they are fair and just.

    Use of Spies and Intelligence¶

    Objective: Gathering information for informed decision-making Why: Information is power. Spies and intelligence networks are crucial for detecting internal and external threats.

    5. Ethics, Dharma & Final Wisdom(normative politics)¶

    (Living in tune with greater truths)

    Dharma and Righteous Conduct¶

    Objective: Living according to moral and cosmic law Why: Dharma is the guiding principle for righteous living and sustaining societal order.

    Impermanence and Death¶

    Objective: Recognizing life’s transitory nature Why: Life, wealth, and relationships are fleeting. Acknowledging impermanence fosters humility and wisdom.

    Detachment and Renunciation¶

    Objective: Freedom from excessive attachment Why: True strength lies in detachment — freedom from attachment to pleasures, possessions, and accolades.

    Wisdom about Fate and Time¶

    Objective: Accepting the inevitability of change and destiny Why: Time is the ultimate force, and wisdom lies in making noble efforts while accepting outcomes with humility before fate.

    • The root of happiness is Righteous conduct
    • The state and its ruler must know their Dharma (proper duty) since all its functions bring happiness when done according to proper knowledge of Dharma.
    • The root of Dharma is finance.
    • A good financial health ensures proper discharge of the duties in a state.The state’s welfare is rooted in good finance.
    • Control over the senses – the feedback from people – is the root of a state’s welfare. The state should formulate its policies according to the feedback it receives from the people with proper modification.
    • Humility is the root of the control of senses of a state. When the state authorities deal with people with humility they get proper and right response from the people.
    • The root of humility is in the service of the seniors – elderly or old persons. When one renders honest service to elders one learns the worth of humility.
    • By serving the old people (or elders) one gets the true knowledge. True knowledge helps the king (or authority) discharge his duties more efficiently.
    • The king who knows his functions or duties well can rule better because he can control his activities judiciously. Hence he gets all prosperity.
    • He who controls his senses has his all wishes fulfilled. He gets wealth and prosperity in every way. Prosperity of the king (the ruling authority) ensures prosperity of people, for if man is prosperous, nature also helps in his prosperity. Even a rulerless kingdom works well if its people are prosperous. [That is, if even a state has no ruler, people if prosperous, can supplement this deficiency by their financial capabilities.]
    • The wrath of the Nature is the worst wrath.
    • It is better to have no king than a king who doesn’t know humility. [An inconsiderate king is worse than having no king.]
    • An able king can train his assistant into efficiency and then he can rule efficiently. [If the king is able he can get a team of his capable assistants and then he can rule well.]Without good assistants a king can take no right decisions. [Meaning the king must be assisted by good assistants if he wants to take right decisions.]

    A single wheel can’t move the chariot. [Indirectly, Chanakya says that alone a king can’t function well. He must get the support of his able cabinet. The king and his cabinet are the two wheels of the state-chariot.]

    The assistant (or minister to a king) must help the master evenly in the latter’s weal and woe. [The assistant shouldn’t desert the master in latter’s misery.]

    • A thoughtful king must weigh pros and cons of a complex problem’s solution within mind before reacting his final decision.

    • Never make a head-strong person your close confidant no matter how dear he / she may be to you. [A head-strong person can be provoked to spill out even the closely guarded secrets. Hence however dear he / she be, that person doesn’t deserve to be made a confidant by the king.]

    • A king should choose only a well-learned and of strong character person as his minister.

    • A minister should have an open mind and unflinchable loyalty to his king.

    • Before starting any project (by the king), long deliberations are indispensable. Success in the project is achieved only when the prior deliberations are held as closely guarded secrets. Leakage of the state’s secrets will destroy the projects. Callousness gets your secrets revealed to your enemy. [Hence the state must guard its secrets securely.] State secrets must be guarded against any possible leakage. [Don’t let your state secrets be passed through any opening. Keep them closely guarded.] Protection of the state’s secrets ensures its continuous prosperity. It is best to keep the state’s secret always closely guarded. Like in darkness the lamp shows the way, so in state administration when the king may feel darkness of confusion, his guide will always be wise deliberations (with his ministers). The king gets the eyes-through deliberations with his cabinet to get a peep into his enemy’s weaknesses. While deliberating upon the state affairs the king must not underestimate his cabinet’s advice or show adamance in forcing his views. When the three sides [that of the king’s view, the advisor’s opinion and the minister’s suggestion] concur on one decision, the deliberations willsurely be crowned with success in any state endeavour. A minister is an able minister only if he can shift clearly through what needs to be done and what not. A secret gets leaked if it falls into six ears. [The state secret must remain with only two persons: the king and the concerned minister. For if a third person – no matter how close – knows about it, it would be out in the open.]

    • A friend is a true friend if he maintains affectionate relations even during the period of your distress. [A friend in need is friend indeed.]

    • A group of friends makes one powerful. [A true friend is defined in the preceding sutra. Such friends group indeed makes one powerful.]

    • A powerful king or man tries to seek the impossible.Only a powerful king can afford to be audacious. Only then he tries to achieve something inaccessible so as to add to his strength and glory.

    • The lazy can’t get any impossible gain. [Since the lazy do not make any effort, it is impossible for them to get any inaccessible advantage.]

    • The lazy can’t protect even the advantage already received.
    • The lazy owing too their lack of efforts can’t ensure the growth of their assets.
    • Lazy kings do not even make their sevants work.

    • It is essential for a state to ensure four inaccessible gains: to get what it doesn’t have; to ensure its security after it is gained; to ensure growth in that asset thus gained; and to swap that gain with something more advantageous to the state.

    • The structure for formulating the home-policy and foreign policy must be an intrinsic part of the state-administration. Home policy must relate to the interior matters of the state. Foreign policy relates to dealing with foreign countries. Pacts and agreements/treaties with other countries is an endless affair. [The king must be aware of his pacts/treaties with foreign countries all the time. Only then can be derived advantage from them at the suitable time.] The king’s competence is judged by not only his formulating apt policies but by also following them sincerely. [The king must not waiver from the policy he has once formulated. For this may tempt his ministers etc. to follow him and thus indiscipline will be bred in the stateadministration.]

    • The countries at the border with which we have frequent skirmishes eventually turn our enemies.

    • Enemy’s enemy becomes our friend. [A common enemy makes even the hostile countries turn friends.]

    • One gets friends or enemies owing to some reason. [Friendship and enmity cannot emerge without any inherent reason.]

    • The weak must not delay in having a peace-treaty with the powerful. The personal strength of two peace makers is the binding cause of a treaty. [When two countries have matching strength and influence, their treaty is a really long lasting treaty.]If a piece of iron is not hot, it won’t join with other iron piece. [Two countries of matchingstrength can be bound by a long lasting treaty.]

    • The powerful should always attack the less powerful. [It is an obvious strategy. Any attack on the powerful will get you generally an adverse consequence.]

    • Never attack someone more powerful or even of matching strength. [One should be cautious ofassessing one’s strength before starting an aggression. Keep postponing any armed conflict till one is ensured of an edge over one’s adversary.]

    • Fighting against more powerful enemy is like foot soldiers taking on the elephant brigade. It would be just a suicidal endeavour!

    • Collision of the two receptacle made of the raw earth results in fragmentation of both. [If two adversaries of immature strength fight with each other, both shall perish.]

    • Always keep on assessing the enemy’s endeavour. [One should always update oneself of one’s enemies activities through secret intelligence and other means. Never take enemy’s strength for granted.]

    • Even if you have a treaty or pact with the neighbouring country, still keep on knowing about its activities through constant surveillance. Always monitor the activities of the enemy’s sleuths. A powerless (or less powerful) king should seek the shelter of a powerful king. Granting shelter to the weak gives much trouble and pain. Seek a king’s shelter with abundant caution like one seeks the shelter of fire. [Fire may burn if you are not cautious and the king may punish if you are careless.] Your behaviour should run counter to the king’s orders.
