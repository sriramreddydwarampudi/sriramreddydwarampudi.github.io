    5 types of indices based on the purpose Got it! Here's a universal explanation of the five types of indices as used across medicine and healthcare, not limited to orthodontics:

    1. Diagnostic Index

    To identify and categorize diseases or conditions in patients based on clinical signs, symptoms, and test results. It helps in standardizing diagnoses and guides clinicians in forming a treatment plan.

    1. Epidemiologic Index

    Used to measure the frequency, distribution, and patterns of diseases in populations. It supports public health planning, monitoring disease outbreaks, and assessing risk factors at a community or national level.

    1. Treatment Need Index

    Determines whether a patient or population requires medical or surgical intervention. It helps in prioritizing care, managing healthcare resources, and ensuring those with the greatest need are treated first.

    1. Treatment Outcome Index

    Used to assess the results of a treatment, including improvements in health, symptom relief, or disease control. It provides a way to evaluate the success or failure of medical interventions and supports evidence-based practice.

    1. Treatment Complexity Index

    Helps in estimating the difficulty or resource-intensity of treating a particular condition. It’s useful for case allocation, insurance planning, and referral decisions, especially when deciding whether a generalist or specialist should manage the case.

    Summary of Purposes:¶

    1. Diagnostic Index → Identify & classify conditions.
    2. Epidemiologic Index → Measure disease frequency in populations.
    3. Treatment Need Index → Prioritize who needs treatment.
    4. Treatment Outcome Index → Evaluate treatment success.
    5. Treatment Complexity Index → Assess difficulty of treatment

    Example in orthodontics 1. Diagnostic index. ex angel's classification 2. Epidemiologic indices ex:summer's occlusal 3. Treatment need ex TPI 4. Treatment outcome indices ex: PAR 5. Treatment complexity index ex: no index yet

    Index of orthodontic treatment need (IOTN)¶

    Key point

    • Only one condition needs to be satisfied to place a patient in that grade.
    • You assign the highest applicable grade based on the most severe feature present.
    • You do not average or combine multiple features.

    Grade 1: No Need for Treatment

    Overjet: <1 mm (normal range) Malocclusion: Extremely minor Displacement: None or negligible

    Grade 2: Little Need for Treatment

    a) Overjet: 3.5–6 mm (with lips competent) b) Reverse overjet: 0–1 mm c) Crossbite: <1 mm discrepancy between retruded contact position (RCP) and intercuspal position (ICP) d) Displacement of teeth: 1–2 mm e) Open bite: 1–2 mm f) Deep bite with no trauma g) Increased overbite with no gingival contact

    Grade 3: Boderline

    a) Overjet: 3.5–6 mm (with lips incompetent) b) Reverse overjet: 1.1–2 mm c) Anterior/posterior crossbite: 1.1–2 mm discrepancy between RCP and ICP d) Displacement of teeth: 2.1–4 mm e) Open bite: 2.1–4 mm f) Deep bite with trauma to palatal or labial mucosa g) Impacted teeth without crowding or displacement

    Grade 4: Need for Treatment

    a) Overjet: >6 mm but ≤9 mm b) Reverse overjet: >2 mm but ≤3.5 mm with masticatory or speech difficulties c) Crossbite: >2 mm discrepancy between RCP and ICP d) Displacement of teeth: >4 mm e) Open bite: >4 mm f) Impacted teeth (excluding third molars) g) Hypodontia requiring pre-restorative orthodontics h) Submerged deciduous teeth i) Craniofacial anomalies (e.g., cleft lip/palate) j) Severe hypoplasia (e.g., enamel/dentine defects)

    Grade 5: Great Need for Treatment

    a) Overjet: >9 mm b) Reverse overjet: >3.5 mm with masticatory or speech difficulties c) Cleft lip and/or palate or other craniofacial anomalies d) Substantial trauma caused by malocclusion (e.g., gingival or palatal trauma) e) Severe hypodontia (requiring extensive restorative treatment) f) Severe skeletal discrepancies

    treatment priority index¶

    By grainger

    1. unacceptable facial aesthetic
    2. Drastic reduction in masticatory function
    3. Speech problems
    4. Unstable occlusion
    5. Occlusal trauma predisposing to tissue damage
    6. Gross defects

    Criteria for measuring malocclusion.

    1. Upper anterior segment overjet
    2. Lower anterior segment overjet
    3. Overbite of upper anterior over lower anterior
    4. Anterior open bite

    5. Congenital absence of incisors

    6. Distal molar relationship

    7. Mesial molar relationship

    8. Buccal cross bite

    9. Lingual cross bite

    10. Intra arch tooth displacements

    11. Gross anomalies

    peer assessment rating¶

    1. Alignment of dentition
    2. Buccal segment relationship
    3. Overjet
    4. Overbite
    5. Midline discrepancy
