Here are the clinical signs for each condition:

1. Abfraction (Caused by occlusal stress and flexural forces)




Wedge-shaped lesions at the cervical third of the tooth

Sharply defined margins

Common on buccal/labial surfaces of premolars and canines

No signs of bacterial involvement (unlike caries)


2. Abrasion (Caused by external mechanical forces like brushing)

V-shaped or notched wear on cervical areas of teeth

Smooth and shiny lesion surfaces

Associated with aggressive tooth brushing or using a hard-bristled brush

Often found on buccal/labial surfaces


3. Caries (Bacterial demineralization of enamel and dentin)

Chalky white spots (early demineralization)

Brown or black cavitations

Soft, sticky texture inside the lesion

May cause pain or sensitivity to sweet, hot, or cold stimuli


4. Attrition (Tooth wear due to tooth-to-tooth contact)

Flattened occlusal or incisal surfaces

Loss of enamel and exposure of dentin

Matching wear facets on opposing teeth

Possible dentin hypersensitivity


5. Hypersensitivity (Exposed dentin leading to pain)

Sharp, transient pain triggered by cold, sweet, or acidic foods

No visible cavitation or decay

Common in cases of gingival recession, abfraction, or erosion

Pain disappears when the stimulus is removed


6. Fracture (Breakage due to trauma or excessive force)

Visible crack or break in enamel, dentin, or pulp

May cause sharp pain during chewing

Sensitivity to temperature changes

Can range from craze lines to complete tooth splitting

== differential diagnosis
abfraction vs abrasion vs erosion

fracture vs attrition vs habit biting

[options="header"]
|===
| Featur  | Abfraction | Abrasion | Attrition | Enamel Fracture
| Cause         | Occlusal stress (bruxism, malocclusion) | Mechanical wear (brushing, habits) | Tooth-to-tooth contact (grinding, chewing) | Sudden trauma (injury, biting hard objects)
| Mechanism     | Flexural stress causes enamel microfractures | Frictional wear removes enamel/dentin | Chronic wear from repeated contact | High-impact force breaks enamel
| Appearance    | Wedge-shaped cervical lesion | Smooth, U- or V-shaped wear | Flat, shiny worn surfaces | Visible cracks, chips, or complete enamel breakage
| Common Sites  | Cervical areas of premolars, molars | Cervical areas of exposed teeth | Incisal/occlusal surfaces of anterior and posterior teeth often generalized | Any tooth, often anterior teeth often localised
| Symptoms      | Sensitivity, pain | Sensitivity to brushing | May be asymptomatic, sensitivity if dentin exposed | Sharp edges, pain, pulp exposure if deep
| Risk Factors  | Bruxism, malocclusion | Hard brushing, abrasive toothpaste, habits | Bruxism, aging, malocclusion | Trauma, weakened enamel, occlusal overload
| Progression   | Gradual with continued stress | Gradual but accelerates with excessive force | Slow and continuous over time | Sudden or progressive if stress persists
| Treatment     | Occlusal adjustments, nightguard, restoration | Soft-bristle toothbrush, habit modification | Occlusal guard, restorations if severe | Bonding, veneer, crown, RCT if pulp exposed
|===
