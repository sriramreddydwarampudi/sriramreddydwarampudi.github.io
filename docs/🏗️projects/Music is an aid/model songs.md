The Taal used in "Jamurathitri" is most closely related to Adi Talam, which is the most common Carnatic Talam.

Adi Talam Structure:

Adi Talam is structured as:

Laghu (1 unit) + Dhrutam (2 units) + Dhrutam (2 units) = 8 aksharas (beats)

The Laghu's length is determined by its jaati — the jaati tells us how many beats are in the Laghu part.

By default, the most common form of Adi Talam is:

Tisra Jaati (3 beats in Laghu) → 3 + 2 + 2 = 7 beats

Chatusra Jaati (4 beats in Laghu) → 4 + 2 + 2 = 8 beats (most common)

Khanda Jaati (5 beats in Laghu) → 5 + 2 + 2 = 9 beats

Misra Jaati (7 beats in Laghu) → 7 + 2 + 2 = 11 beats

Sankeerna Jaati (9 beats in Laghu) → 9 + 2 + 2 = 13 beats


In "Jamurathitri", the rhythm is:

Chatusra Jaati Adi Talam (4-beat Laghu + 2 + 2 = 8-beat cycle) — the most common form, and the one used in most film compositions unless otherwise indicated.
