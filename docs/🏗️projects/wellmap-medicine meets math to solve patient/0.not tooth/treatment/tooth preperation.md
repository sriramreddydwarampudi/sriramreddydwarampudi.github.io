---

Tooth Preparation Principles vs. Steps

A. Fundamental Principles of Tooth Preparation

These are biological and mechanical goals we must achieve:

1. Retention Form – prevents removal along the long axis


2. Resistance Form – prevents tipping or lateral forces


3. Taper – total occlusal convergence (ideally 6–12°)


4. Path of Insertion – imaginary line the crown follows during placement


5. Structural Durability – enough space for restorative material


6. Marginal Integrity – smooth, well-defined margins


7. Preservation of Tooth Structure – minimal but sufficient reduction


8. Biologic Considerations – avoid harming pulp, gingiva




---

B. Clinical Steps of Preparation (How we achieve the above principles):

1. Occlusal Reduction → ensures structural durability


2. Functional Cuspal Bevel → enhances occlusal strength


3. Axial Reduction → for retention + resistance


4. Finish Line Configuration → defines margin (e.g., chamfer, shoulder)


5. Marginal Integrity → smooth, continuous margins


6. Chamfer/Shoulder → specific margin design depending on crown type




---

Summary:

Taper, path of insertion, retention = design principles

Occlusal reduction, axial reduction, chamfer = technical steps to achieve those principles

Here's a simplified breakdown depending on the type of retainer used in a fixed partial denture (bridge):


---


See summary charts in 598 of nallaswamy 

# each type modifications 

1. Complete Cast Crown (Full Metal Crown Retainer)

Preparation: Full coverage crown preparation

Margin: Chamfer

Reduction: 1.0–1.5 mm occlusal, 0.5–1.0 mm axial



---

2. Metal-Ceramic Crown (PFM Retainer)

Preparation: Full coverage with additional reduction for porcelain

Margin: Shoulder (for porcelain) + chamfer (for metal areas)

Reduction: 1.5–2 mm occlusal, 1.2–1.5 mm axial



---

3. All-Ceramic Crown Retainer

Preparation: Full coverage, more reduction for esthetics

Margin: Heavy shoulder or deep chamfer

Reduction: 2 mm occlusal, 1.2–1.5 mm axial



---

4. Conservative Retainers

(Used when minimal reduction is preferred, mostly anterior)

a. Resin-Bonded Bridge (Maryland Bridge)

Preparation: Minimal prep or no prep

Margin: Feather edge or slight chamfer at cingulum/lingual

Reduction: Minimal (mostly enamel for micromechanical retention)


b. Partial Veneer Crown (3/4 or 7/8 crown)

Preparation: Partial coverage of tooth surfaces

Margin: Chamfer or shoulder

Reduction: Varies depending on design
