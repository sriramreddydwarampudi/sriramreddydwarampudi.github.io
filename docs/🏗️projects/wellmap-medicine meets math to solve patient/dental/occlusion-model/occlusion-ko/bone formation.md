
How Bones Are Formed (Bone Formation)

There are 2 ways bones can form in the body:

1. Endochondral Ossification – "Bone replaces cartilage"

First, a soft cartilage model is made.

By differentiation of mesenchymal cells into chondrocytes which forms model

Then, this cartilage model slowly turns into hard bone.

Used for: Long bones like arms and legs.



2. Membranous (Intramembranous) Ossification – "Bone forms directly"

No cartilage here. Bone forms straight from soft tissue (mesenchyme).

mesenchymal cells differentiate into osteoblasts

Used for: Flat bones like the skull and face.


Bone Remodelling – "Old bone is replaced by new bone"

Your bones are always changing. Even after they’re formed, they go through a cycle:

1. Break the old bone – Special cells (osteoclasts) remove weak or damaged bone.


2. Build new bone – Other cells (osteoblasts) fill the space with strong new bone.



This keeps bones strong and healthy!
