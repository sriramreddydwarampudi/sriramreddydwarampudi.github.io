Great question! There are four main types of yoga in Indian philosophy—each is a different path to liberation (moksha), depending on your personality and life approach. Think of them as different roads to the same destination: union with the Divine or Self-realization.


---

1. Karma Yoga – The Path of Action

For: People who are active and duty-oriented.

Focus: Selfless service, doing your duty without attachment to results.

Goal: Purify the ego and reach liberation through action.


Why?
Because constant action can lead to spiritual growth when it's done without selfishness or desire for reward.

> “Do your duty, offer the fruits to God.” – Bhagavad Gita




---

2. Bhakti Yoga – The Path of Devotion

For: Emotional and loving personalities.

Focus: Love, surrender, and devotion to a personal deity (like Krishna, Shiva, Devi, etc.).

Goal: Union through pure love and surrender.


Why?
Because love dissolves the ego, and devotion fills the heart with peace and joy.

> “Offer me your heart, your love, your sacrifice, and I will deliver you.” – Bhagavad Gita




---

3. Jnana Yoga – The Path of Knowledge

For: Philosophical, reflective minds.

Focus: Study of scriptures, self-inquiry, and discrimination between real (Self) and unreal (body, world).

Goal: Realize the true Self as pure consciousness (Atman = Brahman).


Why?
Because ignorance is the root of bondage, and knowledge leads to freedom.

> “Know that by which all this is known.” – Upanishads




---

4. Raja Yoga – The Path of Meditation (Patanjali Yoga)

For: Those who seek mastery over mind and meditation.

Focus: Mind control, meditation, eight-limbed path (Ashtanga Yoga).

Goal: Experience Samadhi (absorption in pure awareness).


Why?
Because the mind is the instrument of bondage and liberation—master it, and you're free.

> “Yoga is the stilling of the mind’s movements.” – Yoga Sutras




---

Bonus Paths:

Hatha Yoga – Focuses on physical postures and breath to prepare for meditation.

Kundalini Yoga – Awakens the dormant spiritual energy at the base of the spine.

Mantra Yoga – Uses sound vibrations (like Om) for mental purification.
