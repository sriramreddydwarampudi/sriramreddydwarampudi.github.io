Understanding textual representation or language is a complex cognitive process that involves multiple factors, including linguistic knowledge, cognitive abilities, context, and prior experience. Here's a breakdown of how we understand language, why some people may struggle with it, and the types of understanding:

### 1. **How We Understand Textual Representation or Language**
Understanding language involves several interconnected processes:

#### a. **Linguistic Comprehension**
   - **Syntax**: Understanding the structure of sentences and the relationships between words.
   - **Semantics**: Grasping the meaning of words and sentences.
   - **Pragmatics**: Interpreting language in context, considering the speaker's intent, tone, and situation.

#### b. **Cognitive Processing**
   - **Working Memory**: Holding and manipulating information while reading or listening.
   - **Attention**: Focusing on relevant details and filtering out distractions.
   - **Inference**: Drawing conclusions based on implicit information or filling in gaps in the text.

#### c. **Contextual Understanding**
   - **Prior Knowledge**: Using existing knowledge to make sense of new information.
   - **Cultural Context**: Recognizing cultural references, idioms, and social norms that influence meaning.

#### d. **Emotional and Social Cues**
   - **Empathy**: Relating to the emotions or perspectives of others in the text.
   - **Social Context**: Understanding how social dynamics influence language, such as politeness, formality, or power relations.

### 2. **Why Some People Can't Understand Language**
Various factors can lead to difficulties in understanding language:

#### a. **Language Disorders**
   - **Dyslexia**: A learning disorder that affects reading and language processing.
   - **Aphasia**: A condition resulting from brain injury that impairs language comprehension or production.
   - **Specific Language Impairment (SLI)**: Difficulty with language skills that isn't caused by a general developmental disorder.

#### b. **Cognitive Impairments**
   - **Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD)**: Challenges with sustained attention can make it difficult to follow complex texts.
   - **Memory Issues**: Problems with working memory can affect the ability to hold and process information.

#### c. **Linguistic Challenges**
   - **Limited Vocabulary**: Not knowing enough words to understand the text.
   - **Complex Syntax**: Difficulty with complex sentence structures or unfamiliar grammatical patterns.
   - **Second Language Learners**: Non-native speakers may struggle with nuances, idioms, or cultural references.

#### d. **Environmental Factors**
   - **Distractions**: Noise, multitasking, or interruptions can hinder comprehension.
   - **Poor Quality Text**: Ambiguous, poorly written, or overly complex text can be hard to understand.

#### e. **Emotional and Psychological Factors**
   - **Anxiety**: Stress or anxiety can interfere with focus and comprehension.
   - **Lack of Motivation**: Disinterest or disengagement can lead to a lack of understanding.

### 3. **Types of Understanding**

#### a. **Literal Understanding**
   - **Definition**: Grasping the explicit meaning of words, sentences, and text without interpreting any deeper meaning.
   - **Example**: Understanding a news report by focusing solely on the facts presented.

#### b. **Inferential Understanding**
   - **Definition**: Drawing conclusions or making inferences based on implicit information, context, or prior knowledge.
   - **Example**: Understanding a character's motives in a story based on their actions and dialogue, even if not directly stated.

#### c. **Critical Understanding**
   - **Definition**: Analyzing, evaluating, and questioning the information presented, often comparing it with other knowledge or perspectives.
   - **Example**: Evaluating the credibility of an argument in an editorial by considering evidence, logic, and potential biases.

#### d. **Evaluative Understanding**
   - **Definition**: Judging the value, credibility, or significance of the information or argument presented in the text.
   - **Example**: Assessing whether a scientific study's conclusions are supported by the data and methodology used.

#### e. **Reflective Understanding**
   - **Definition**: Connecting the text to personal experiences, values, or broader philosophical questions, leading to deeper insight or self-reflection.
   - **Example**: Reflecting on how a novel's themes relate to one's own life experiences or beliefs.

#### f. **Creative Understanding**
   - **Definition**: Interpreting the text in imaginative ways, seeing new connections, or generating new ideas based on the content.
   - **Example**: Writing a creative piece inspired by themes or ideas in a poem.

#### g. **Contextual Understanding**
   - **Definition**: Recognizing the influence of context, such as historical, cultural, or situational factors, on the meaning of the text.
   - **Example**: Understanding the political undertones in a historical document by considering the era in which it was written.

#### h. **Emotional Understanding**
   - **Definition**: Connecting with the emotional content of the text, recognizing the feelings and attitudes expressed by the author or characters.
   - **Example**: Feeling empathy for a character in a novel and understanding their emotional journey.

### Summary
Understanding language is a multi-faceted process that involves cognitive, linguistic, and contextual elements. The ability to comprehend text varies widely among individuals due to factors such as cognitive abilities, linguistic knowledge, and environmental influences. Different types of understanding, from literal to reflective, contribute to how we process and interpret textual representations, making it a rich and complex aspect of human cognition.
