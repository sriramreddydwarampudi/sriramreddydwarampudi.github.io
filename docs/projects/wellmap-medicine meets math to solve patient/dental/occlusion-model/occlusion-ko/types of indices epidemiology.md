5 types of indices based on the purpose 
Got it! Here's a universal explanation of the five types of indices as used across medicine and healthcare, not limited to orthodontics:


---

1. Diagnostic Index


To identify and categorize diseases or conditions in patients based on clinical signs, symptoms, and test results. It helps in standardizing diagnoses and guides clinicians in forming a treatment plan.


---

2. Epidemiologic Index


Used to measure the frequency, distribution, and patterns of diseases in populations. It supports public health planning, monitoring disease outbreaks, and assessing risk factors at a community or national level.


---

3. Treatment Need Index


Determines whether a patient or population requires medical or surgical intervention. It helps in prioritizing care, managing healthcare resources, and ensuring those with the greatest need are treated first.


---

4. Treatment Outcome Index


Used to assess the results of a treatment, including improvements in health, symptom relief, or disease control. It provides a way to evaluate the success or failure of medical interventions and supports evidence-based practice.


---

5. Treatment Complexity Index


Helps in estimating the difficulty or resource-intensity of treating a particular condition. It’s useful for case allocation, insurance planning, and referral decisions, especially when deciding whether a generalist or specialist should manage the case.




---

### **Summary of Purposes:**  
1. **Diagnostic Index** → Identify & classify conditions.  
2. **Epidemiologic Index** → Measure disease frequency in populations.  
3. **Treatment Need Index** → Prioritize who needs treatment.  
4. **Treatment Outcome Index** → Evaluate treatment success.  
5. **Treatment Complexity Index** → Assess difficulty of treatment

# Index of orthodontic treatment need (IOTN)

Key point

- Only one condition needs to be satisfied to place a patient in that grade.
- You assign the highest applicable grade based on the most severe feature present.
- You do not average or combine multiple features.

---

Grade 1: No Need for Treatment

Overjet: <1 mm (normal range)
Malocclusion: Extremely minor
Displacement: None or negligible



---

Grade 2: Little Need for Treatment

a) Overjet: 3.5–6 mm (with lips competent)
b) Reverse overjet: 0–1 mm
c) Crossbite: <1 mm discrepancy between retruded contact position (RCP) and intercuspal position (ICP)
d) Displacement of teeth: 1–2 mm
e) Open bite: 1–2 mm
f) Deep bite with no trauma
g) Increased overbite with no gingival contact



---

Grade 3: Boderline

a) Overjet: 3.5–6 mm (with lips incompetent)
b) Reverse overjet: 1.1–2 mm
c) Anterior/posterior crossbite: 1.1–2 mm discrepancy between RCP and ICP
d) Displacement of teeth: 2.1–4 mm
e) Open bite: 2.1–4 mm
f) Deep bite with trauma to palatal or labial mucosa
g) Impacted teeth without crowding or displacement



---

Grade 4: Need for Treatment

a) Overjet: >6 mm but ≤9 mm
b) Reverse overjet: >2 mm but ≤3.5 mm with masticatory or speech difficulties
c) Crossbite: >2 mm discrepancy between RCP and ICP
d) Displacement of teeth: >4 mm
e) Open bite: >4 mm
f) Impacted teeth (excluding third molars)
g) Hypodontia requiring pre-restorative orthodontics
h) Submerged deciduous teeth
i) Craniofacial anomalies (e.g., cleft lip/palate)
j) Severe hypoplasia (e.g., enamel/dentine defects)



---

Grade 5: Great Need for Treatment

a) Overjet: >9 mm
b) Reverse overjet: >3.5 mm with masticatory or speech difficulties
c) Cleft lip and/or palate or other craniofacial anomalies
d) Substantial trauma caused by malocclusion (e.g., gingival or palatal trauma)
e) Severe hypodontia (requiring extensive restorative treatment)
f) Severe skeletal discrepancies



---
