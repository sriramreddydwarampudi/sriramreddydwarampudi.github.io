Methods of Gaining Space in Orthodontics

I. With Reduction of Tooth Material

1. Proximal Slicing

Definition: Reduction of mesiodistal crown width to gain minor space (≤2.5 mm).

Synonyms: Slenderization, Reproximation, Interproximal Reduction (IPR), Proximal Stripping/Disking.

Indications: Mild crowding, Bolton discrepancy.


Steps:

1. Diagnosis & Planning

Carey’s Analysis: Measures arch length deficiency.

Bolton Analysis: Identifies tooth size discrepancy.

Diagnostic Set-up: Locates site and amount of reduction.

IOPA Radiograph: Assesses enamel and pulp.

Peck & Peck Ratio: Assesses tooth proportion.



2. Proximal Reduction

Types: Localized (usually anterior), Generalized.

Instruments: Abrasive strips, safe-sided discs, tapered burs.

Precaution: Max 40% enamel reduction; maintain contact areas.

Technique: ARS (Air-Rotor Stripping) – sequential posterior to anterior.



3. Post-op Care

Fluoride application: Prevents sensitivity and caries.




Advantages:

Avoids extractions.

Enhances interarch relationships.

Stabilizes contact areas.


Drawbacks:

Risk of caries, sensitivity.

Esthetic compromise.

Gingival irritation if overdone.



2. Extraction

Used when space requirement is significant.

Strategic removal of teeth (e.g., premolars) to relieve crowding.





---

II. Without Reduction of Tooth Material

1. Arch Expansion

Widening of dental arch to create space.



2. Labial Proclination

Forward movement of anterior teeth.



3. Distalization

Backward movement of molars (e.g., with headgear, mini-implants).



4. Derotation

Correcting rotated teeth to align properly and gain space.



5. Uprighting

Correcting tilted posterior teeth, improving occlusion and space.

Preventive Orthodontics

Goal: Maintain normal occlusion by preventing the development of malocclusion.

Without Appliances

Parent education – Informing parents about normal dental development and habits to prevent malocclusion.

Oral hygiene & caries control – Preventing premature tooth loss or infections that could affect occlusion.

[Habit correction] – Eliminating harmful oral habits (e.g., thumb sucking, mouth breathing) early.

Timely extraction of retained/malpositioned teeth – Removing deciduous teeth that delay or alter permanent tooth eruption.

Monitoring eruption patterns – Regular check-ups to ensure teeth erupt in correct sequence and position.


With Appliances

Mouth guards – Protect teeth from trauma in children involved in contact sports.

Space maintainers – Appliances used to preserve space after premature loss of a primary tooth.



---

Interceptive Orthodontics

Goal: Detect and eliminate developing malocclusions in early stages.

Occlusal equilibration – Selective grinding of tooth surfaces to correct minor bite discrepancies.

Correction of crossbite (e.g., tongue blade therapy) – Using simple tools like tongue blades to push misaligned anterior teeth into position.

Habit-breaking appliances – Devices used to discourage habits like thumb sucking, tongue thrusting, or lip biting.

Serial extraction – Planned sequence of tooth extractions to guide eruption and alignment of permanent teeth.

Space regainers – Appliances that recover lost space due to premature tooth loss or drifting.

Muscle exercises – Myofunctional therapy to correct tongue posture, swallowing, and orofacial muscle tone.

Early Invisalign – Using clear aligners in mixed dentition stage to correct minor developing malocclusions.



---

Surgical Orthodontics

Goal: Correct severe skeletal or dental problems using surgical procedures in conjunction with orthodontics.

Minor Procedures

Frenectomy – Removal of an abnormal frenum (e.g., labial or lingual) that may cause spacing or restriction.

Surgical exposure of impacted teeth – Surgically uncovering teeth that are trapped in the bone/gums to aid eruption.

Corticotomy – Cutting cortical bone to accelerate orthodontic tooth movement.

Pericision – Cutting periodontal fibers around the tooth to reduce relapse after correction.

Therapeutic/supernumerary extractions – Removal of extra or problematic teeth that interfere with normal occlusion.

Removal of cysts/soft tissue barriers – Eliminating obstacles like cysts or fibrous tissue that block eruption.

Orthodontic mini-implants (TADs) – Small temporary screws placed in bone to provide anchorage for tooth movement.


Major Procedures

Orthognathic surgery – Repositioning of jaws to correct severe skeletal discrepancies (e.g., underbite, open bite).

Cleft lip/palate repair – Surgical reconstruction of cleft defects to restore function and aesthetics.

Cosmetic jaw surgeries – Surgeries to improve facial appearance, often combined with orthodontic correction.

Surgically assisted maxillary expansion (SARME) – Breaking the mid-palatal suture to widen the upper jaw in adults.

Distraction osteogenesis – Gradual lengthening of bone using a device after surgically cutting the bone.
